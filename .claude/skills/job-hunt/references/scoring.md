# Scoring fit

Fit is a holistic 0-100 judgement of how well *this person* matches *this job*, with a reason
and gaps. It is not a keyword score. Be honest; a flattering score wastes their morning.

## Weights (judgement guide, not arithmetic)

| Factor | Weight | Look at |
|---|---|---|
| Skills & domain overlap | 3 | Required skills/experience they evidence vs. lack; sector familiarity |
| Seniority & scope | 2 | Level, team/account size, ownership vs. their track record |
| Role-type alignment | 2 | Matches the roles in `current-search-focus.md` (and what they say they want less of) |
| Practical fit | 2 | Location/remote, pay vs. floor, company type, deal-breakers (a deal-breaker caps fit at 30) |
| Evidence of relevant wins | 1 | Achievements in the CV that map to what the role needs |

Bands: 75+ strong, 55-74 worth a look, below 55 stretch. Don't inflate to hit a band.

## Writing the reason and gaps

- `fit_reason`: one line, specific ("Enterprise AM scope matches your last two roles; pay band is
  below your floor"). No "great opportunity" filler.
- `gaps`: only real ones from the posting (missing skill, level mismatch, location). If pay or
  anything else is unknown, say unknown rather than assuming.
- With no CV/role context yet, score on search focus only, cap at 60, and say "provisional".

## Learning from outcomes

Only after enough outcomes exist (roughly 10+ resolved):

- Same-day or <48h rejections → likely automated screening: check keyword coverage in the CV
  versus postings.
- Rejections after interviews → not a CV issue; suggest interview prep.
- Rejections clustered by role type/company type/seniority → narrow targeting.
- Many unrelated role types → too broad.
- No clear pattern → say so (could be normal variance). Never invent a cause.
- Always state what you changed and why before applying it to later searches.
