# Cover letter playbook

How every cover letter (and the written questions on application forms) is built. Set up 8 Nov 2026 after Harry asked
for a structure we can replicate. Facts still come only from the master profile.

## The structure: four paragraphs, 250 to 350 words

| # | Paragraph | Job it does | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Why this role** | The hook, and why this role is the right next step | 2 to 3 sentences. Three sentences: a true, plain observation about the company's world (never "I want"), Harry's relevance in one line, then why this role now with any personal hook (M-Pesa) as the reason it's genuine. No unverified client name-drops, and don't use the angle that paragraph 3 owns. |
| 2 | **Why me** | Two or three proofs mapped to the top "What you'll do" points | One proof per job description point, each with its "so what". Tell the story behind a CV line, don't repeat the CV. Write it as connected sentences with transitions ("With Dyson…", "Just as important…"), never a list ("Three things… I… I… And I…"). |
| 3 | **Why this company** | One specific, researched reason, tied to Harry's own work | Name a product, strategy or stated value, with a source. Never generic praise. The honest gap goes here, framed with evidence. |
| 4 | **The value I'd add, and close** | What he'd do in the first months, then a call to action | Concrete and in the posting's words. One closing line inviting a conversation. |

Research (Nov 2026) agrees on four short paragraphs, roughly 250 to 400 words, a specific "why this company" rather than generic
praise, linking experience to the job instead of repeating the CV, and a confident close. Most evidence is career-service
and expert guidance rather than hard recruiter data.

## The flow

1. **Decode the posting.** List the "What you'll do" and "Who you are" points (the same list the CV rating uses).
2. **Research the company.** Find one specific thing (a product, a strategy, a stated value, recent news) with a source.
   If the site is blocked, use search or ask Harry. No research, no paragraph 3.
3. **Pick the blocks.** The hook (why this role), two or three proofs for the top points (why me), the company reason
   (why this company), and one value statement (what I'd do first).
4. **Draft the four paragraphs**, 250 to 350 words.
5. **Gauntlet.** Truth check against the master profile. Map each paragraph to the job description points it answers
   (record the map in the working doc). AI-tell check (no semicolons, no ≈ or ~, no colons joining clauses, no stock
   phrases reused from another letter). Read aloud for simple, natural sentences. Word count.
6. **Harry reviews** in the working doc. Lessons go into the learning journal.
7. **Reuse for application forms.** "Motivation" questions come from paragraphs 1 and 3. "Why are you a good fit"
   comes from paragraphs 2 and 4, cut to one key message.

Harry often sends the CV only. The letter is still worth building, because its blocks become the form answers.

## Retired habits

The earlier letters (Oct to Nov 2026) ran to five or six paragraphs and 400 to 500 words, reused the same M-Pesa
opening and "build a working answer this afternoon" line for every company, listed CV numbers rather than explaining
them, and had a weak "why this company" because company sites were often blocked. Don't reuse a stock line across
letters to different companies.
